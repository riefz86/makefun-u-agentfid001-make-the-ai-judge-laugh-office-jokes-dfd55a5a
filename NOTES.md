# Drafting notes — Make the AI Judge Laugh: Office Jokes

## The brief, in my own words
One line about office life, under 200 characters, with a setup and a
turn, punchline last. The judge weighs, in order: a real one-line joke
about office life; a turn on something anyone who has worked in an
office recognises; surprise; every word earning its place; freshness.

## Angles I considered and rejected
- "This meeting could have been an email" — the single most obvious
  office joke. The brief itself names it as an example, so half the
  field will submit some version of it. Rejected for freshness.
- The reply-all disaster and the printer that jams on deadline day —
  both named in the brief as examples too. Same reason: rejected.
- Monday morning and the photocopier — named in the brief as the
  obvious lines. Rejected for freshness.
- The pizza party instead of a raise ("my salary is now paid in
  pepperoni and exposure") — decent turn, but the "paid in exposure"
  gag belongs to freelancers more than office workers, so the
  recognition is weaker. Rejected.
- "We circled back so many times the meeting room got a frequent-flyer
  program" — cute, but the turn leans on the jargon itself rather than
  on a truth about the office; the laugh is a smile, not a wince.
  Rejected.
- The office thermostat (arctic tundra vs meeting-room sauna) — the
  setup is relatable but the turn ("facilities calls that climate
  strategy") explains the joke instead of landing it. Rejected.

## The angle I kept
The shared drive's pile of "final" folders. Everyone who has worked
in an office has seen FINAL, FINAL_v2 and FINAL_v2_REAL side by side,
and the turn — the only thing the office ever finishes is naming
things — is a truth that stings a little. The setup is concrete, the
punchline is the last word group, and no word needs explaining.

## Revision
First draft listed three folder names. Revised to four
(FINAL, FINAL_v2, FINAL_v2_REAL, FINAL_v2_REAL_FINAL): three names
set the pattern, the fourth breaks it, so the absurdity lands harder
before the punchline, without adding a single explanatory word.

## Final line (145 characters)
> Our shared drive has FINAL, FINAL_v2, FINAL_v2_REAL and FINAL_v2_REAL_FINAL folders -- the only thing this office ever finishes is naming things.
